How to use the Hazardous Materials Assessor

Follow these steps in order. You stay in control. The app keeps the records. AI is used only when you export a batch and send it yourself.

Quick path (most projects)
  1. Upload SDS PDFs in the Bulk workspace.
  2. Confirm product names and page ranges.
  3. Export an AI batch of 25, get the JSON back, and merge it.
  4. Download the owner workbook, send it for quantities, then import the returned file.
  5. Export Excel and review MAQ results. Finish HMIS notes if needed.

Step 1 — Start the project

You do:

  • Open the Bulk SDS workspace.
  • Enter the project name, jurisdiction, code basis (IBC or NFPA 400), and edition.
  • Name the delivery (example: “Building A — owner SDS — Nov 2026”).
  • Upload the SDS PDFs or a ZIP of PDFs. Wait for the reading progress to finish.
  • Export the receipt log and check that every file was registered.

The app stores the files in this browser only. It does not send them anywhere. Keep the original PDFs as a backup.

Step 2 — Check the products

You do:

  • Open each source and confirm the product name, manufacturer, and revision.
  • For a single SDS, accept the suggested page range if it covers the whole sheet.
  • For a combined PDF, set the correct page ranges so every page is assigned once.
  • Use “Review next 25” for simple files. Fix any unreadable text before continuing.

The app creates a permanent ID for each material. Later results and quantities will match on that ID. Do not rename or delete IDs.

Step 3 — Classify hazards with AI (25 at a time)

You do:

  • Click Select next 25 without results.
  • Click Export selected AI batch. A ZIP downloads.
  • Give the ZIP (and any required code text) to your AI. Follow the AI-INSTRUCTIONS.txt inside the ZIP.
  • When the AI returns a JSON file, use Preview AI results, review the changes, and apply the merge.
  • Repeat until every material has results or an open question.

Stay at 25 materials per batch (50 maximum). The app never runs AI itself. You control what is sent and what comes back.

Step 4 — Get quantities from the owner

You do:

  • Click Download owner workbook.
  • Send the workbook to the person who knows the site quantities.
  • They fill in storage, closed-use, and open-use amounts, locations, and on-site status. They keep the prefilled IDs. They leave unknowns blank and enter 0 only when they are sure the amount is zero.
  • When the file comes back, use Load returned Excel / CSV, review the preview, and apply the import.

This step is separate from classification. Do it after the hazards are finished.

Step 5 — Review MAQ and export

You do:

  • Export the assessment Excel (or JSON).
  • Open the Excel and check the MAQ Tables sheet. Rows marked YES exceed the limit for that hazard and location.
  • Turn on the cabinet or sprinkler toggle for any material that uses an approved cabinet or has sprinkler coverage. Re-export if you change flags.
  • Confirm control areas and any Group H implications yourself. The app shows the numbers; you make the final call.

Do not add repeated hazard rows together as inventory totals. Unique materials are listed on the Material inventory sheet.

Step 6 — Finish HMIS notes (if needed)

You do:

  • Open Assessment / HMIS and import the assessment JSON if it is not already loaded.
  • Assign materials to rooms or control areas.
  • Review or edit the room notes. Validate the rooms when they are correct.
  • Export the reviewed HMIS text and paste the blocks into Revit text notes.

Nothing is final until a person validates it. The app prepares the text; you confirm it matches the plans and quantities.

Important rules

  • One workspace per project. You can upload more packages into the same workspace later.
  • Set the code edition before you export any AI batch.
  • AI batches: 25 is best, 50 is the maximum.
  • PDF limit: 100 MB per file. ZIP limit: 1 GB expanded. Split larger deliveries.
  • Export a complete backup ZIP after each major step. Browser storage can be cleared.
  • Human review is required at every stage. The app does not issue final engineering conclusions.
