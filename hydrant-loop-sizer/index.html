Hydrant Loop Sizer

Version 1.1.0

Code basis: —

Sizes a private hydrant loop (6, 8, 10, 12, 16 in.) from a flow test, or checks a fire pump and tank that feeds the same loop. Flow test: NFPA 291 available flow, ring hydraulics, hydrant arms, velocity, single-break case and pressure class. Pump and tank: pump curve (churn, rated, and 150% flow), tank low-water suction, a supply main, and a lead-in with a backflow-preventer loss at each sprinkler riser. Friction loss is Hazen-Williams with the actual inside diameter (NFPA 24, 2019 or 2022). Flow-test mode is hydrant-only supply. Pump mode checks the entered riser demand because the hydrants share the loop.

Units

All calculations run in US units internally (1 in. = 25.4 mm, 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 gpm = 3.785411784 L/min = 0.2271247 m³/h, 1 psi = 6.894757 kPa = 0.06894757 bar). Switching units converts every value shown; nothing is rounded in storage.

Supply

Flow test sizes a hydrant-only loop. Pump and tank checks the discharge pressure a fire pump needs at the sprinkler risers, including the supply main, the loop, each lead-in, and the backflow preventer on that lead-in.

Code basis

Flow test (NFPA 291)

Elevations default to 0 (level). Elevation head is 0.433 psi per ft.

A flow test whose pressure drop is under the warning threshold gives an unreliable extrapolation. This tool defaults to 10% (its spec); the Flow Test Report app uses 25%. Enter 25 to match it, or leave the default of 10.

Fire pump and tank

Net pressure is a straight line from churn at 0 gpm, through the rated point, to the pressure you enter at 150% of rated flow. Churn cannot be over 140% of rated pressure, and the 150% point cannot be under 65% of rated pressure (NFPA 20 s6.2). Nothing is filled in for you: enter the certified points, or enter the 140% and 65% limits if you are checking the minimum curve. Tank low-water suction is added to the net pressure. Suction may be zero or negative.

Supply main (pump to the loop)

The supply main carries the full riser demand from the pump discharge to one connection on the loop. It stays in service in the loop-break case. Loop length is entered with the loop below. Hazen-Williams uses the actual inside diameter for the selected pipe, not the nominal size.

Sprinkler risers

One lead-in from the loop to each riser. Every sprinkler riser on this project has a backflow preventer, because the hydrants share the loop. Enter that preventer's pressure loss on the lead-in (zero only when the data says zero). The riser requirement is the pressure needed at the base, after the lead-in and the preventer.

Hydrant arms (laterals)

Each hydrant has its own arm. Size defaults to 6 in. (NFPA 24 minimum) and length is optional (blank or 0 means no arm friction is added). An arm left at the default size, or with no length, is marked "assumed, confirm" in the printout. Arm friction and arm velocity go into each hydrant's residual check.

Examples

Loop length, hydrant count and pipe in the SEA example are illustrative: the flow test (68.3 / 61.2 psi at 1,250 gpm) is the only given data. The pump example is a 2,500 gpm pump at 115 psi with suction at 0, a 360 ft supply main, a 2,321 ft loop, and 2,602 gpm at a riser that needs 106.4 psi through 150 ft of lead-in. Churn and the 150% point are entered at the NFPA 20 limits (140% and 65%). Backflow loss is 0 for that check.

How this tool works

  1. Choose the supply. Hydrant flow test uses static, residual and flow. The tool prints the NFPA 291 available flow at the minimum residual and builds the supply curve. Fire pump and tank uses the pump curve instead of a flow test.
  2. Flow test: enter the required fire flow and the hydrants flowing, or use the table lookup and hydrant suggestion for the chosen code basis. Pump and tank: enter rated flow and pressure, churn (not over 140% of rated pressure), the pressure at 150% of rated flow (not under 65% of rated pressure), and the tank low-water suction pressure.
  3. Flow test: enter the loop length, connections, pipe and the arm of each hydrant. The tool checks 6, 8, 10, 12 and 16 in. pipe for velocity, residual pressure at every hydrant (arm friction included), a single-break case and the pressure class.
  4. Pump and tank: enter the supply main from the pump to the loop, the loop size and length, and each riser. Every sprinkler riser has its own lead-in and a backflow-preventer loss, because the hydrants share the loop. Friction loss is Hazen-Williams with the actual inside diameter. Pick NFPA 24 2019 or 2022; both editions use the same formula here. The report gives normal operation and a loop-break case (one loop segment out, demand not reduced): required pump discharge, pressure available at the demand, PASS or FAIL, and the margin.
  5. The pump curve is a straight line between the three points you enter. It is not extended past 150% of rated flow. Confirm it against the certified factory curve. Items marked "confirm with AHJ/edition" were not verified in the code text. They are listed on the report.
