Standpipe Calculator

v1.0.11 · NFPA 14-2019 Compliant

User Guide

This calculator complies with NFPA 14-2019. It determines the pressure required at the base so the hydraulically most remote outlet meets the residual and flow requirements of the standard. Friction uses the Hazen-Williams formula.

  • Class — Select I, II, or III. Class I and III require 100 psi residual at the most remote 2½ in. outlet. Class II requires 65 psi at the most remote 1½ in. outlet (NFPA 14-2019 7.8).
  • Number of standpipes — Total standpipes in the system. Flow is 500 gpm for the first + 250 gpm for each additional, capped at 1,000 gpm (sprinklered) or 1,250 gpm (unsprinklered). Class II is fixed at 100 gpm (7.10).
  • Building sprinklered? — Yes if the building is sprinklered throughout per NFPA 13. This sets the system flow cap (1,000 gpm vs 1,250 gpm) per NFPA 14 7.10.1.
  • Height to top outlet (ft) — Vertical distance from the base (pump or FDC) to the most remote outlet. Used for elevation loss at 0.433 psi per foot.
  • Pipe diameter (in) — Internal diameter of the riser or main being calculated. NFPA 14 minimum is 4 in. for Class I/III standpipes and 6 in. for combined systems.
  • Pipe length (ft) — Length of the pipe segment (riser or main) from the base to the top outlet. Convert fittings to equivalent length and add if desired.
  • C-factor — Hazen-Williams roughness coefficient. Use 120 for wet steel (most common), 100 for dry systems, or 140 for cement-lined or smooth pipe.

Result: Pressure required at base = residual at top (7.8) + elevation + friction loss (Hazen-Williams). Provide at least this pressure at the base so the top outlet has the required residual. All steps are referenced in the on-screen results and the printed report.
