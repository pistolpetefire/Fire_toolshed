Screening only. Not the submittal basis. Use FDS (NIST Fire Dynamics Simulator) or another AHJ-accepted method for the submittal. A licensed fire protection engineer must confirm the inputs, the design fire, the applicability of each correlation, and the tenability criteria with the AHJ.

Exhaust (optional)

Exhaust is treated as removing layer gas at the layer temperature (no plugholing, no makeup-air velocity check). While the exhaust exceeds the plume supply, no layer accumulates (thin-layer floor of 1 mm, equilibrium temperature). The NFPA 92 filling equations do not apply with exhaust; the numerical model governs.

Tenability criteria

Base default 6 ft (1.83 m) above the highest occupied floor; margin is your adjustable allowance. Confirm both with the AHJ.

    User guide

    How to choose every input, what the outputs mean, and how to hand ASET to the Transparent RSET Tool. Values below are typical starting points from the cited sources, not design values. Where values vary, they are per project and AHJ. Confirm the editions your AHJ has adopted.

    Workflow

    1. Hold an AHJ kickoff: agree on the design fire(s), the tenability criteria, the margin, the required ASET/RSET ratio, and the submittal method (FDS or another accepted method).
    2. Choose units, then enter the space geometry (ceiling height, floor area, fuel elevation).
    3. Enter the design fire (type, growth class or steady HRR, cap) and its citation.
    4. Enter exhaust (if any), ambient temperature and the tenability criteria.
    5. Read every warning. Fix any input that sits outside a correlation range, or plan to use FDS for that case.
    6. Run the sensitivity cases agreed with the AHJ (faster growth, higher cap, exhaust impaired) and record each one.
    7. Enter RSET (Detection, Notification, Pre-movement, Movement), or import an RSET Tool session JSON, and read the ASET − RSET margin.
    8. Click Export RSET Tool session. In the Transparent RSET Tool, use Load Session; the governing ASET arrives as its tenability limit.
    9. Export the project JSON and print the report for the file. Then build the submittal analysis in FDS or another AHJ-accepted method.

    Reading the warnings

    • Error (red): inputs are missing or out of range, or the criterion is undefined. Nothing is computed until you fix them.
    • Warning (amber): a correlation is outside its validity range (A/H², z/H below 0.2, HRR cap reached before the algebraic ASET, flame height near the criterion or the ceiling, very tall space), or the citation is missing. Rely on the numerical result or FDS for that case and record why.
    • Note (gray): information, such as exhaust disabling the algebraic equation, a criterion not reached within the end time, non-default χc or λ, and the reminder that visibility, toxicity and heat flux are not evaluated.

    Common mistakes

    • Using the NFPA 92 filling equation after the HRR cap is reached, or with exhaust running.
    • Picking a growth class from the occupancy name without looking at the actual fuel (for example, high-piled storage in a "business" building).
    • Leaving the design-fire citation blank, or citing a value you cannot trace to a table or test report.
    • Measuring heights from the floor when the fire is elevated (enter the fuel elevation).
    • Applying the 6 ft criterion at the main floor when people are on a higher balcony or mezzanine open to the space.
    • Treating a "not reached" result as infinite ASET without checking the end time and the equilibrium height.
    • Raising the heat-loss fraction without a basis, which makes the layer thinner and cooler.
    • Treating this screen as the submittal; it does not evaluate visibility, toxicity, radiation, stratification or non-uniform geometry.

    Method

    All calculations run internally in SI. Heights in the correlations are measured from the base of the fire (fuel elevation); H and z on screen are measured from the floor.

    1 · NFPA 92 filling equations (no exhaust, uniform cross-section)

    Steady: z/H = 1.11 − 0.28 ln[ (t Q^(1/3) / H^(4/3)) / (A/H²) ] valid 0.9 ≤ A/H² ≤ 14, z/H ≥ 0.2
    t-squared: z/H = 0.91 [ t / (t_g^(2/5) H^(4/5) (A/H²)^(3/5)) ]^(−1.45) valid 0.9 ≤ A/H² ≤ 23, z/H ≥ 0.2

    z = layer interface height above the fire base [m]; H = ceiling height above the fire base [m]; t [s]; Q = HRR [kW]; tg = growth time to 1,055 kW [s]; A = cross-sectional area [m²]. Source: NFPA 92, Standard for Smoke Control Systems, smoke-filling equations (Annex/Chapter on calculation procedures); SFPE Handbook of Fire Protection Engineering, 5th Ed., smoke control / smoke-filling chapters (Milke). Confirm the edition adopted by the AHJ. The t-squared form is invalid after the HRR cap is reached.

    2 · NFPA 92 axisymmetric plume

    z_l = 0.166 Q_c^(2/5); z > z_l: m = 0.071 Q_c^(1/3) z^(5/3) + 0.0018 Q_c; z ≤ z_l: m = 0.032 Q_c^(3/5) z [kg/s, kW, m]

    Qc = χc·Q (default χc = 0.7). Fire assumed away from walls and corners (axisymmetric). Balcony spill, window and wall/corner plumes are not modeled.

    3 · Two-zone mass/energy-balance layer model (numerical)

    dM/dt = m_p(z) − ρ_L V̇_e dV_L/dt = [ m_p c_p T₀ + (1 − λ) Q_c ] / (c_p ρ₀ T₀) − V̇_e z = H − V_L / A, T_L = ρ₀ T₀ V_L / M

    Constant-pressure upper layer, ideal gas (ρT = ρ₀T₀), cp = 1.005 kJ/kg·K, ρ₀ = 101,325/(287.05·T₀). Plume entrainment from section 2 evaluated at the current interface height. λ = fraction of convective heat lost to boundaries (λ = 0 is adiabatic: deepest, hottest layer). Fourth-order Runge–Kutta, Δt = 0.1 s. Same physics as classic two-zone fill models (e.g., SFPE Handbook zone-model chapters; CFAST technical reference), simplified to a single compartment with no vents other than exhaust.

    4 · Tenability and ASET

    ASET (layer) = first time the interface reaches (base height + margin) above the floor. Optional ASET (temperature) = first time TL reaches the limit (numerical model only). Governing ASET = the earliest applicable criterion from the governing method (the numerical model; the NFPA 92 algebraic result is shown alongside for comparison and is not used to govern). Visibility, toxicity (FED) and radiation are not evaluated: use FDS or another AHJ-accepted method.

    Limits

    • Uniform cross-section, flat ceiling, single well-mixed upper layer; no stratification, no smoke-layer lag in tall spaces, no ceiling-jet transport delay.
    • No sprinkler interaction (cooling, HRR control) unless reflected in your cited design fire and cap.
    • No leakage, no door openings, no makeup air; exhaust plugholing and inlet velocities are not checked (see NFPA 92).
    • The NFPA 92 filling equations are empirical: outside 0.9 ≤ A/H² ≤ 14 (steady) or 23 (t²), or below z/H = 0.2, results are flagged.
    • Not a visibility, toxicity, or heat-flux tenability analysis. Not the submittal basis.
