Methodology

I = Σ (qty × device alarm current)

Leff = k × Lone-way (k = 1 end, 0.5 distributed, 0.15 start). Class A adds the return length.

VD = 2 × I × (Ω/kft ÷ 1000) × Leff using NEC Ch. 9 Table 8 stranded copper.

Vlast = Vsource − VD. Default source is 20.4 V (85% of 24 V). Fail if Vlast < Vmin (default 16 V) or I > NAC rating.

Entire circuit current is applied over each row’s length (conservative). Not a node-by-node listed calc.

Speakers: W = Σ (qty × tap), Ipair = W / Vaudio (25 V or 70.7 V). Vmin = 85% of Vaudio. Remote-amp 24 V current: I = idle + W / (η × 24).

Battery: Ah = (Istby·tstby + Ialarm·talarm) × aging × temp × spare. Standby 24 h (NFPA) or 60 h (UFC). Alarm 5 min horn-strobe, 15 min NFPA voice, or 60 min UFC 4-021-01 MNS at max connected load.

Notification circuits

NAC = 24 V horns/strobes. Audio = speaker taps. A speaker-strobe is two loads (strobe on a NAC, tap on audio). Distance is one-way from that circuit’s source.

How to find device details (candela, mA, taps, distance)

Type — Match the plan symbol: H/S = horn-strobe, S = strobe, SPKR = speaker, SP/ST = speaker-strobe (enter on both a NAC and an audio circuit).

Candela (Cd) — Strobe brightness from the NFPA 72 room-size tables, or the value already noted on the lighting/FA plan. 15 cd is a small room; 75 cd is a common corridor start. Brighter = more milliamps.

mA — Open the device cut sheet. Use the 24 VDC alarm-current row for your candela. If the sheet says 0.165 A, type 165. Until you have the sheet, leave the typical value and note “replace at submittal.”

Speaker tap (W) — Often written next to the speaker on the drawing (1W, 2W). Higher tap = louder = more amplifier load. 1 W is a common interior start if nothing is shown. Confirm with the voice-evac layout later.

Qty — Count devices that share the same type, candela or tap, and roughly the same wire length on this circuit. Split the row if any of those change.

Ft — One-way cable length along the route (corridors and shafts, not through walls). After you add a booster, measure from the booster, not the old homerun to the FACP. When unsure, enter a longer distance (safer).

AWG — 16 is typical. 14 or 12 if the run is long. This app will not treat 10 AWG as a recommended fix — use a booster instead.

Place — Distributed = devices along a corridor. End = treat the load as at the far end (conservative). Start = clustered at the panel.
