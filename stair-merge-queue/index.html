    Per-floor results

    Clearance = last occupant of that floor enters the stair. Door queue delay = clearance minus the uncongested door time (ready + people / door capacity). Max queue = most people waiting at that floor's stair door.

    RSET handoff

    Movement = last exit of that floor's occupants minus their start of movement (delay + pre-movement). Pre-movement for the RSET Tool is taken from the floor row.

    User guide

    What each input is, typical starting values, and why. Values are starting points from the cited sources, not design values. Where values vary they are per project and AHJ.

    Workflow

    1. Agree with the AHJ on the scenarios (phased and simultaneous, loss of one stair), the merge assumption, and how movement time will be used in RSET.
    2. Enter the stairs: number, clear widths, edge condition, door widths into the stair and at discharge, riser/tread, floor-to-floor height and landing travel.
    3. Enter each floor from the top down: occupants, phased notification delay, pre-movement and walking distance to the stair door. Add the basis for these values.
    4. Choose the merge rule and the stair to lose for the loss case.
    5. Read the warnings, the per-floor table and the chart. Compare phased, simultaneous and loss cases.
    6. In RSET handoff, choose the floor (for example the event floor) and case, enter detection and notification, and export the RSET Tool session. Load it in the Transparent RSET Tool with Load Session.
    7. Export the project JSON and print the report. Confirm with an AHJ-accepted egress model for the submittal.

    Reading the warnings

    • Error (red): invalid inputs (missing, non-numeric, widths smaller than the boundary layers, loss case with one stair). Nothing is computed.
    • Warning (amber): a door or discharge bottleneck, a missing citation, zero pre-movement, very tall stacks, or a case where phasing increases total evacuation time.
    • Note (gray): modeling assumptions, such as even split between stairs and no counterflow.

    Common mistakes

    • Using nominal instead of clear width, or forgetting the boundary layers.
    • Ignoring merging flow from floors below the floor of interest.
    • Leaving out landings, or using the vertical rise as the travel distance.
    • Assuming phased evacuation always shortens total time.
    • Using the hydraulic result as a full RSET: it covers movement only, for an idealized able-bodied crowd.
    • Treating the even-split or merge rule as fact rather than an assumption to bracket.

    Method

    1 · SFPE hydraulic model

    S = k (1 − a·D), a = 0.266 m²/person (D in persons/m²); S = 0.85 k for D below 0.54; Fs = S·D
    Maximum specific flow at D = 1/(2a) = 1.88 p/m²: Fsm = k/(4a); speed at Fsm = k/2
    Effective width We = clear width − boundary layers; capacity C = Fsm · We [persons/s]

    k: corridor, ramp, doorway 1.40; stairs 7.5/10: 1.00; 7/11: 1.08; 6.5/12: 1.16; 6.5/13: 1.23. Travel along stairs = rise × conversion factor (1.66, 1.85, 2.10, 2.22 for those stairs) + landing travel. Source: SFPE Handbook, 5th Ed., Ch. 59 (Gwynne and Rosenbaum). Confirm your edition's tables.

    2 · Time-step merge and queue model

    Each stair is a chain of story segments. Flow leaving a landing reaches the next landing down after the story travel time (at the speed for maximum flow, k/2). At each landing the stair capacity C·Δt is shared between people arriving from above and the floor's door queue, by the selected merge rule. Floor entry is also limited by the door capacity (Fsm,door · We,door, k = 1.40). The discharge door capacity, if lower, is applied as the stair capacity. People are tracked by floor of origin. Δt = 0.5 s.

    3 · Cases

    Phased: each floor starts at delay + pre-movement + walk time. Simultaneous: delay = 0 for all floors. Loss of one stair: phased case with the selected stair removed and its share redistributed evenly.

    Limits

    • Idealized able-bodied crowd: no slow tail, no people with mobility impairments, no assisted evacuation, no elevators.
    • No counterflow (firefighters), no fatigue, no behavior at merges beyond the rule chosen, no route choice (even split between stairs).
    • Queues inside the stair from a discharge bottleneck are represented by reducing stair capacity, not by tracking density along flights.
    • Floors below the discharge level and the discharge-level floor itself are not modeled.
    • Movement only. Not a tenability analysis and not the submittal basis.
