FIRE TOOLSHED / USER GUIDE

Size the service.
Keep the basis visible.

This tool estimates a lead-in and a water-filled riser from a known flow and pressure demand. For systems without a pump, it also provides a provisional backflow connection size or checks an entered manufacturer curve. It is not a complete sprinkler hydraulic calculation or a code-compliance certificate.

Choose your starting point

Quick estimate is for early sizing before hydraulic calculations exist. Enter an estimated riser flow, or select Hazard classification under either NFPA 13 or UFC. The result uses pipe inside diameters, minimum sizes and velocity targets only. It does not verify pressure, supply capacity, delivery time or actual hydraulic demand. Density × area is a theoretical flow before hydraulic over-discharge; allow for the uncertainty in your estimate.

Known demand checks the pressure budget using calculated flow and required pressure. Enter those values or choose Load calculated demand to load a JSON summary in the format shown in the demand template. The file must identify its pressure reference as system-side-riser, excluding the upstream service losses modeled here. PDF calculation reports are not automatically parsed; transfer their correctly referenced summary values manually.

Common controls stay on the main panel. Expand Advanced for pipe catalogs, hose distribution, flow-test data, pump mode, fitting lengths and manufacturer curves. Selecting indoors/outdoors or horizontal/vertical updates the illustration and guidance; it does not invent pipe lengths, elevations or additional fittings. Update those when you know the actual route. Vertical requires a listed model and an accepted installation; selection in this app is not approval.

1. Start with the demand location

  1. Enter the project, chosen criteria and system type. All initial numbers are examples, not verified project values.
  2. Enter the most demanding riser's flow, including inside hose that actually passes through that riser. The required outlet pressure must exclude the lead-in, BFP, riser, other device allowance and reserve calculated by this app. If the source demand already includes these losses, rebase it first.
  3. Place exterior hose flow at its actual branch. “Upstream” does not flow through the service lateral; “lead-in only” branches before the BFP. This app has one riser and does not solve multiple simultaneous branches.
  4. Enter residual supply pressure at the evaluated total flow, or a static/residual/flow test. Flow-test extrapolation is identified. A test is not proof of future utility capacity.
  5. Enter pipe lengths, equivalent fitting lengths, elevation changes, materials, C factors and project minimum sizes. Use the product's actual inside diameter basis. The PVC catalog uses nominal DR18 geometry; confirm manufacturer dimensions.
  6. Enter BFP and other device loss. Keep a pressure reserve. Review the sizes, pressure ledger and every item to confirm.

2. What the sizes mean

The company rule constrains the size pairs before the engine checks velocity and, in Known demand, pressure. The engine tries nominal sizes 2½, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 inches, with PVC starting at 4 inches. It chooses the smallest passing lead-in, then the smallest passing riser. This is a reproducible estimating order, not an installed-cost optimization. Project minimum sizes are user inputs; the default 6-inch lead-in is not presented as a universal code minimum. NFPA 24 and other applicable standards can impose additional requirements.

The BFP connection size matches the lead-in. Company sizing pairs are riser/lead-in: 4/6, 6/8, 8/10, 10/10 and 12/12 inches (smaller pairs are possible only if project minimums allow). This upsize preference is not an NFPA or UFC requirement. Without a manufacturer curve, the matching BFP size remains provisional. Pipe velocity does not establish a BFP's rated capacity or pressure loss. With a curve, the entered model and connection size are retained; the engine interpolates pressure loss only within the supplied flow range. That assembly connection size also constrains the matching lead-in; the app will not silently select another BFP model. Verify listing, orientation, hazard classification, valve arrangement and manufacturer data. Include reducer losses when connection sizes differ.

3. Criteria applied

UFC 3-600-01, September 30, 2026 is the frozen UFC version used in this build; see the official WBDG edition listing. The selected NFPA basis is NFPA 13 (2022). Confirm adoption and project exceptions; this pairing is the user's requested project basis.

ComponentApplied check
UFC service lateral · §9-310 ft/s maximum. A smaller user target can govern.
UFC inlet · §9-3.220 psi minimum at BFP inlet or pump suction, whichever is nearest the main.
Pump lateral · §§9-3, 9-5.4150% rated pump flow for the service check; suppression demand limited to 140% rating.
UFC BFP · §9-6.2.3At least 8 psi DC or 12 psi RP; the larger entered/curve loss governs.
Interior riserUser planning velocity target; no fabricated universal NFPA 13 velocity cap.

UFC nonstorage demand helper: Table 9-1 and §9-7.4 group wet, single-interlock and noninterlock together; dry, double-interlock and deluge use the dry criteria. Ceiling height and hazard affect the density/area estimate. Above 60 feet the helper rejects the dry group. It cannot establish design demand for storage, hangars or special hazards; sprinkler K-factor, response type, distribution and hydraulic over-discharge must be resolved separately. Hose allowance and duration follow Table 9-2. Remote-head pressure is never substituted for calculated riser pressure.

NFPA 13 is the hydraulic design framework, not an automatic pipe-size lookup by system name. Full standard text was not bundled into this tool. The app does not check floor-area-per-riser limits, dry-system volume/delivery time, coverage, obstruction rules, sprinkler selection, seismic details or special-occupancy requirements. Establish those before relying on the service estimate. Do not substitute this tool for a licensed standard or project fire protection analysis.

Orientation and relief-drain guidance must follow the selected model. See, for example, manufacturer installation instructions; permitted orientations vary by model and size. UFC §9-6.3 also limits horizontal assembly bottom height to 24 inches above the floor and the upper handwheel of a vertical assembly to 6 feet. Confirm clearances and freeze protection.

Hazard-based flow estimates

Select NFPA 13 or UFC, then choose Hazard classification as the estimate method. Light, OH1, OH2, EH1 and EH2 are available. NFPA presets use the basic new-system density/area option in Table 19.2.3.1.1; dry and double-interlock estimates increase area by 30%. Quick-response reductions, alternative design points and room design are not applied. NFPA presets are limited here to nonstorage standard-spray systems with ceilings up to 30 feet; deluge requires manual project demand. UFC uses its own Table 9-1, ceiling bands and system groups, including its minimum K-factor. OH1/OH2 and EH1/EH2 share UFC group criteria.

Automatic NFPA K-factors and coverage are estimating selections, not blanket approval of a sprinkler: confirm listing, spacing, temperature, response and applicable minimum K rules. A larger K-factor reduces pressure needed for a given head flow, but the minimum operating-pressure floor can increase discharge. Above 60 feet, UFC response/listing requirements also remain applicable.

Number of heads = ceil(design area / assumed coverage per head)
Head flow = max(density × coverage, K × sqrt(minimum head pressure))
Remote-head pressure = (head flow / K)²
Estimated riser flow = ceil(head count × head flow × (1 + allowance/100))

The default 20% allowance is an estimating choice, not a code multiplier or a substitute for hydraulic balancing. Coverage defaults are 150 ft²/head for light, 130 for ordinary and 100 for extra; verify the actual installation rules. Remote-head pressure is not copied into the required riser pressure. Exterior/inside hose allocation remains an independent input in Advanced.

Source checks: National Fire Academy 2022-edition training material, PDF page 33 reproduces the new density/area options; National Fire Academy hydraulic-calculation training explains head flow and pressure relationships. Consult the adopted standard for the final design.

4. Equations and pressure accounting

v = 0.4085 Q / d²                            [ft/s]
ΔP = 4.52 Q^1.85 L / (C^1.85 d^4.87)          [psi]
ΔP elevation = 0.433 × rise                   [psi]
P supply = Ps − (Ps − Pr) × (Qtotal / Qtest)^1.85

No pump:
P inlet = P supply − lead friction − lead elevation
P outlet = P inlet − BFP − riser friction − riser elevation − devices
Remaining = P outlet − required outlet pressure − reserve

Q is gpm, d is inside diameter in inches, L is actual plus equivalent fitting length in feet. Negative elevation is downhill and adds pressure. Both modeled pipe segments are water-filled. C=120 is the example steel input, not a universal material or condition assignment; confirm the correct factor. Do not apply a nitrogen benefit from a later NFPA edition to the 2022 basis without justification.

In pump mode the lead-in is evaluated at the greater of 150% rated pump flow or the entered lead-in demand. Upstream hose is then added at the supply. The riser is evaluated at suppression demand from the separately entered discharge pressure, after any discharge-side BFP. BFP selection and net pump head are deliberately outside that mode. The suction pressure input is a project requirement: perform a separate NPSH and NFPA 20 evaluation. No automatic reduced-flow waiver is applied.

“Insufficient pressure before pipe friction” means larger pipe alone cannot solve the entered pressure budget or inlet requirement. “No size combination” means no available size satisfies all entered limits; inspect candidate results. A passing combination is always a preliminary estimate. It does not verify maximum working pressure, surge, supply reliability or equipment approval.

5. Printouts and logos

Select Benham, MeadHunt JV, AI&P + Benham, a Haskell state-specific lockup, or upload a logo. Selection uses the same Toolshed logo storage key as the existing apps. Choose Print / Save PDF, then your browser's printer or PDF destination. The printout retains assumptions and unresolved items. “Save report” downloads a standalone HTML report with embedded logo and diagram; open it in a full browser to print or share it. Some embedded app browsers do not expose a print dialog. Blank or invalid calculation fields block printing. Expand candidate sizes before printing only if you want that table included.

On iPad or iPhone, open the deployed HTTPS page in Safari; use the browser print/share controls as needed. Do not depend on opening an HTML file from the Files app. The interface stacks on small screens and the schematic scrolls horizontally to preserve readable labels. Device-specific printing can differ; verify the PDF preview. Desktop browser QA does not replace physical iPad/iPhone testing.

6. Save, import and handoff

Valid inputs autosave locally in your browser. Storage may be unavailable in private mode or with restrictive settings. Export JSON for a portable record, then import it to recalculate on another device. Nothing is uploaded by this app. A new origin (such as a local preview) cannot read another site's saved Toolshed values.

“Use Toolshed demand” reads the existing sprinkler estimator handoff on the same origin. It imports sprinkler-only flow and puts hose upstream by default. Confirm inside-hose allocation. It clears required pressure so the upstream estimator's pressure stack cannot be accidentally counted twice. This app's JSON contract is separate from the shared Toolshed project schema.

7. Bot interface

Humans and bots use the same pure engine. In a browser, call window.FireServiceSizer.evaluate(input). In Node:

const fs = require('node:fs');
const engine = require('./engine.js');
const input = JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync('sample-input.json', 'utf8'));
console.log(JSON.stringify(engine.evaluate(input), null, 2));

The complete input contract is schema.json; sample-input.json contains an example. All original hydraulic fields are required, including inactive-mode fields, so outputs are deterministic and defaults are never silently substituted. The optional hazardEstimate object contains hazard (light/oh1/oh2/eh1/eh2), heightFt, kFactor (null for preset), coverageFt2 (null for preset), minPressurePsi and allowancePct. In Quick estimate it determines demand; in Known demand it is ignored. Results expose the full derivation as hazardCalculation. Other optional fields are sizingMode (quick/known; legacy default known), bfpLocation (indoor/outdoor; default indoor), and bfpOrientation (horizontal/vertical; default horizontal). Quick results have pressureChecked: false and null pressure/friction results. Result statuses: quick-estimate, invalid, estimate, no-fit, insufficient-supply, no-friction-budget, pump-demand-limit. Always inspect errors, warnings and selected; an estimate is not a construction approval. Results include candidate sizes, losses, velocities and pressure margins. Imports ignore stored results and recalculate.

BFP curve format:

{"model":"Your manufacturer and model","sizeIn":6,
 "points":[[0,4],[500,8],[1000,12]]}

Those points illustrate the format only; they are not product performance data. Use published data for the actual assembly. No network, account, DOM or logo assets are required by the engine. The UI exposes stable form IDs matching input keys and labeled controls for browser automation. No service worker is installed, avoiding stale engine/UI cache combinations.

Storage and ESFR planning

Choose Hazard classification, then a planning band or ESFR 40 ft roof / 35 ft storage. The 1,100 / 1,300 / 1,400 gpm bands are arbitrary budget steps, not code hazard classes. The ESFR planning example assumes 12 K25.2 heads at 25 psi (1,512 gpm before the editable allowance). Confirm the applicable NFPA 13 (2022) storage table and sprinkler listing. No commodity selection or storage-design compliance check is performed.

FM comparison uses DS 8-9 Table 8, wet QR K25.2 pendent, 40 ft maximum roof: 9 heads at 40 psi. The app retains UFC service checks when UFC is selected; its nonstorage density table is not used for these storage examples. FM minimum hose is allocated upstream, less any hose assigned to the lead-in; it is not added twice. Inside hose and in-rack combinations require Known demand. Gross flow × duration is not usable tank capacity and gives no credit for refill.

Source: FM DS 8-9, Tables 8 and 14; §2.3.5. Product applicability: TYCO TFP312, December 2024. TFP312 refers standard storage designs to NFPA/FM; it does not independently validate this app’s NFPA budget scenario.

Bot input: hazardEstimate.hazard accepts budget1100, budget1300, budget1400 and esfr40. Optional fm:true selects the FM scenario for esfr40 only. Storage presets ignore nonstorage height, coverage, K and pressure-floor fields; their fixed assumptions appear in the result. Known demand ignores retained hazard estimates.
